# Vehicle physics research (2026-11-14)

Supporting evidence for [0118](../0118-vehicles.md). Each claim cites a source fetched on this date.
Unverified items are listed at the end. Values in the "Shard reading" lines are interpretation.

## 1. Engines and their data model

Every engine below models the chassis as one rigid body and each wheel as a scene query along a
suspension axis, plus a spring-damper and a tire force at the contact. None uses a jointed wheel body.

### Unreal Chaos Vehicles (UE5)

- Components: `UChaosWheeledVehicleMovementComponent` on the pawn and one `UChaosVehicleWheel` class
  per wheel. Engine, transmission and differential configs sit on the movement component.
- Wheel ground query: `SweepShape` is `Raycast` (default, fastest), `Spherecast`, or `Shapecast` (the
  wheel's collision shape, slowest). `SweepType` is `SimpleSweep` or `ComplexSweep` (simple or complex
  collision). https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/ChaosVehicles/ESweepShape ,
  https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.27/API/Plugins/ChaosVehicles/ESweepType/index.html
- Wheel fields, with defaults mirrored by a third-party wrapper of `UChaosVehicleWheel`
  (https://rapyutasimulationplugins.readthedocs.io/en/devel/doxygen_generated/html/d7/d09/struct_f_r_r_robot_wheel_property.html):
  `CorneringStiffness` 1000, `FrictionForceMultiplier` 2, `SideSlipModifier` 1 ("lateral skid grip
  loss"), `SlipThreshold` / `SkidThreshold` 20, `LateralSlipGraph` (a float curve with slip angle on
  x), `SuspensionMaxRaise` / `MaxDrop` 10 cm, `SuspensionDampingRatio` 0.5, `WheelLoadRatio` 0.5 (0 =
  load-independent friction, 1 = fully load-based), `SpringRate`, `SpringPreload`, `RollbarScaling`
  0.15, `SuspensionSmoothing`, `MaxSteerAngle` 50°, `MaxBrakeTorque` 1500, `MaxHandBrakeTorque` 3000,
  `bABSEnabled`, `bTractionControlEnabled`.
- Tire model: a simple stiffness-and-friction model, not Pacejka. `LateralSlipGraph` units are
  undocumented. Users ask about them at https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/chaos-vehicles-lateral-slip-graph-units-and-calculation-principle-normalized-vs-absolute/2728830
- Per-surface friction: a tire-config friction scale per physical material. The UE4 PhysX
  `UTireConfig::SetPerMaterialFrictionScale` is documented at https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.27/en-US/API/Plugins/PhysXVehicles/UTireConfig/SetPerMaterialFrictionScale/index.html
  The Chaos equivalent is unverified (see the last section).
- Drivetrain: `EVehicleDifferential` is AllWheelDrive, FrontWheelDrive or RearWheelDrive, with
  `FrontRearSplit` for AWD (https://docs.unrealengine.com/4.27/API/Plugins/ChaosVehicles/FVehicleDifferentialConfig/index.html).
  The transmission has `ChangeUpRPM`, `ChangeDownRPM`, `GearChangeTime`, `TransmissionEfficiency`
  and `FinalRatio`, plus forward and reverse ratios. Automatic shifts pass through neutral
  (https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/ChaosVehiclesCore/FSimpleTransmissionConfig).
  Chaos has no PhysX-style autobox latency, and users report gear skipping
  (https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/chaos-vehicle-skipping-gears/1995441). The engine has a
  `TorqueCurve` normalized against `MaxTorque`, plus `MaxRPM` and `EngineIdleRPM`.
- Chaos Modular Vehicles has native support for the Network Physics component and the resimulation
  replication mode (https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/chaos-modular-vehicles-overview).
  Forum reports from 5.5–5.8 describe client/server mismatches in collisions and resimulation loops.
  An Epic staffer calls networked physics experimental
  (https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/ai-controlled-vehicles-stop-working-when-physics-prediction-is-enabled/2835577).

### Unity WheelCollider (Unity 6)

- One `WheelCollider` per wheel under a `Rigidbody`. Its friction is "computed separately from the
  rest of physics engine", and PhysicMaterial is ignored
  (https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.2/Documentation/ScriptReference/WheelCollider.html).
- Fields: `suspensionSpring` (`JointSpring` spring, damper, `targetPosition`), `suspensionDistance`,
  `sprungMass`, `ResetSprungMasses()`, `forceAppPointDistance` (where suspension and tire forces
  apply, measured from the wheel base), `wheelDampingRate`, `mass`, `motorTorque`, `brakeTorque`,
  `steerAngle`, `suspensionExpansionLimited`, `GetGroundHit`, `GetWorldPose`. The probe is a ray only.
- `WheelFrictionCurve`: a curve from (0,0) to the extremum (`extremumSlip`, `extremumValue`), falling
  to the asymptote (`asymptoteSlip`, `asymptoteValue`) and flat after it. `stiffness` multiplies
  both values. That is five numbers per axis
  (https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.2/Documentation/ScriptReference/WheelFrictionCurve.html).
  Inspector defaults: extremum 0.4/1, asymptote 0.8/0.5, stiffness 1, spring 35000 N/m, damper
  4500, targetPosition 0.5, distance 0.3 m, wheel mass 20 kg
  (https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.2/Documentation/Manual/class-WheelCollider.html).
- `ConfigureVehicleSubsteps(speedThreshold, stepsBelowThreshold, stepsAboveThreshold)` splits each
  fixed step and computes suspension and tire forces per substep. Call it once per vehicle
  (https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.2/Documentation/ScriptReference/WheelCollider.ConfigureVehicleSubsteps.html).
- **Vehicle Physics Pro (Edy)** measures slip as a **velocity in m/s**, not a ratio. Its curve has
  Adherent, Peak and Limit points: 0.95 below 0.5 m/s, a peak of 1.1 at 1.5 m/s, 0.8 from 4 m/s.
  Adherent friction is for a stopped, braked car and fades out by `maxAdherentSpeed` (23 km/h).
  Forward slip reduces lateral grip (combined slip). Pacejka sets need conversion
  (https://vehiclephysics.com/blocks/tires/). Its differentials are Open, Locked, Viscous, Clutch Pack
  (preload, friction, power and coast ramp angles) and Torque Bias (preload, power and coast ratios
  n:1, "4:1 Torsen = 0 preload, both ratios 4") (https://vehiclephysics.com/blocks/differential/).
- **NWH Vehicle Physics 2** detects the surface per wheel, with a friction curve, sound and effects
  per surface. Its friction presets use a modified Pacejka (B, C, D, E). Its modules include ABS,
  TCS and ESC. The transmission is Automatic, Automatic Sequential, Manual, CVT or External.
  Differentials are Open, Locked, LSD or Torsen. A stalling clutch and 15+ sound effects are
  included (https://nwhcoding.com/VehiclePhysics/).

### PhysX 5 vehicle (formerly "vehicle2"; headers now under `physx/include/vehicle/`, PhysX 5.6–5.13)

- Pipeline: `PxVehicleComponent::update(dt, context)` components run in a
  `PxVehicleComponentSequence`. `beginSubstepGroup(n)` / `endSubstepGroup()` substep any subset, and
  groups can nest. The PhysX actor begin/end components bracket the sequence
  (https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.6.1/docs/Vehicles.html).
  `PxVehicleRigidBodyComponent` "forward integrate[s] the momentum and pose of the vehicle's rigid
  body", so a substep group can include the chassis (`PxVehicleRigidBodyComponents.h`,
  github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/PhysX).
- Tire model: linear forces first (`longStiff * longSlip`, `camberStiff * camber`, lateral stiffness
  from a load graph `latStiffX` / `latStiffY` ≈ 2–3 / 100000), then the combined force is limited by
  load × friction with smoothing. `frictionVsSlip[3][2]` is a 3-point friction-vs-longitudinal-slip
  graph that scales the road friction. `loadFilter` smooths normalized load, which helps at large
  timesteps. `restLoad` (`PxVehicleTireParams.h`).
- Friction per surface: `PxVehicleRoadGeometryState::friction` holds "the friction value for the
  combination of surface type and tire type". The PhysX integration fills it per material.
- Low speed: `PxVehicleTireSlipParams` sets minimum slip denominators. The 60 Hz defaults are
  `minLatSlipDenominator` 1, `minActiveLongSlipDenominator` 0.1 and `minPassiveLongSlipDenominator`
  4 (m/s). "Larger timesteps typically require larger values." The **sticky tire**: a tire that
  stays below `thresholdSpeed` for `thresholdTime` "without application of drive torque" switches to
  velocity constraints that asymptotically approach zero. Defaults are 0.2 m/s and 1 s on both axes,
  damping 1 longitudinal and 0.1 lateral (`PxVehicleParams.h`, `PxVehicleTireFunctions.h`).
- Anti-roll: `PxVehicleAntiRollBar {wheel0, wheel1, stiffness}`. The torque is proportional to the
  difference in spring displacement. Ackermann: `PxVehicleAckermannParams {wheelIds[2], wheelBase,
  trackWidth, strength}`, where 1 is ideal correction.
- Drivetrain: multi-wheel differential (`torqueRatios` sum to 1), four-wheel differential and tank
  differential. In the four-wheel one, **`frontBias`, `rearBias` and `centerBias` are maximum
  wheel-speed ratios, not torque ratios** ("1.3 is a good starting point"). The `*Target` ratios sit
  just below them (1.29), and `rate` sets how fast a breach is corrected. Clutch:
  `eBEST_POSSIBLE` (an implicit solve, recommended for 4 wheels) or `eESTIMATE` with
  `estimateIterations` (for 20-wheel trucks). Gearbox: `ratios` (neutral 0, reverse negative),
  `finalRatio`, `switchTime`. Autobox: `upRatios` / `downRatios` against `maxOmega`, and a `latency`
  "well above switchTime". Engine: `torqueCurve` over normalized rpm, `moi`, `peakTorque`, `idleOmega`,
  `maxOmega`, and three damping rates (full throttle, zero throttle with the clutch in or out).
- Sprung masses: the sprung masses must reproduce the body's mass and center of mass.
  `PxVehicleComputeSprungMasses(n, coords, totalMass, gravityDir, out)` projects wheel coordinates
  onto the ground plane (COM at the origin). It solves exactly for 1–3 wheels. For 4 or more it uses
  Lagrange multipliers to minimize `Σ(mᵢ − m̄)²` subject to the two COM equations and total mass
  (`VhSuspensionHelpers.cpp`). It does not clamp negative masses.
- Suspension guidance: `stiffness = ω² m_sprung`, where "a typical family car" has ω "between 5 and
  10" (in rad/s, given the formula: 0.8–1.6 Hz). Damping ratio is "between 0.8 and 1.2 … a good
  starting point". Keep `sqrt(m/k)/dt > 5`. Reference timestep 60 Hz.

### Jolt `VehicleConstraint`

- A constraint on the chassis, registered as a step listener. Controllers: `WheeledVehicleController`,
  `MotorcycleController` and `TrackedVehicleController`. Collision testers:
  `VehicleCollisionTesterRay` (with `inMaxSlopeAngle` 80° to ignore walls), `…CastSphere` and
  `…CastCylinder` (`inConvexRadiusFraction` 0.1)
  (https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/master/Jolt/Physics/Vehicle/VehicleCollisionTester.h).
  `mMaxPitchRollAngle` limits how far the chassis can tip.
- `WheelSettings`: `mSuspensionSpring` (frequency and damping, **default 1.5 Hz, 0.5**), min and max
  length 0.3 and 0.5 m, preload, optional `mSuspensionForcePoint` (applying at the contact is "more
  accurate with dynamic objects but less stable"), radius, width
  (https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/class_wheel_settings.html).
- `WheelSettingsWV`: `mInertia` 0.9, `mAngularDamping` 0.2, `mMaxSteerAngle` 70°, `mMaxBrakeTorque`
  1500, `mMaxHandBrakeTorque` 4000. `mLongitudinalFriction` is a piecewise-linear curve of slip ratio
  (default (0,0), (0.06,1.2), (0.2,1.0)). `mLateralFriction` is a curve of slip angle in degrees
  (default (0,0), (3,1.2), (20,1.0)).
- Tire forces are **constraint parts with impulse limits**. The solver drives relative contact
  velocity to zero within its velocity iterations, clamped by `TireMaxImpulseCallback`, which by
  default is `friction × suspensionImpulse` on each axis independently, with no friction circle.
  A brake that can stop a wheel within the step locks it outright. The slip denominator floor is
  1e-3 m/s. Surface friction combines with tire friction through a callback, by default the
  geometric mean `sqrt(f1·f2)` (`WheeledVehicleController.cpp/.h`, `VehicleConstraint.h`).
- `VehicleAntiRollBar {mLeftWheel, mRightWheel, mStiffness 1000 N/m}`. Engine: `mMaxTorque` 500,
  `mMinRPM` 1000, `mMaxRPM` 6000, `mNormalizedTorque` curve, `mInertia` 0.5, `mAngularDamping` 0.2.
  Transmission: Auto or manual, ratios {2.66, 1.78, 1.3, 1.0, 0.74}, reverse −2.90, `mSwitchTime` 0.5,
  `mClutchReleaseTime` 0.3, `mSwitchLatency` 0.5, `mShiftUpRPM` 4000, `mShiftDownRPM` 2000,
  `mClutchStrength` 10.
- `VehicleDifferentialSettings`: `mDifferentialRatio` 3.42, `mLeftRightSplit` 0.5,
  `mEngineTorqueRatio`. **`mLimitedSlipRatio` 1.4 is "Ratio max / min wheel speed. When this ratio is
  exceeded, all torque gets distributed to the slowest moving wheel"**. `FLT_MAX` makes it open.
  `mDifferentialLimitedSlipRatio` 1.4 applies the same rule between differentials
  (https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/class_vehicle_differential_settings.html).

### Rapier `DynamicRayCastVehicleController` (@dimforge/rapier3d-compat 0.20.0)

- Created with `world.createVehicleController(chassis)`. The source says it is "ported and modified
  from Bullet's `btRaycastVehicle`" (https://github.com/dimforge/rapier/blob/master/src/control/ray_cast_vehicle_controller.rs).
- API (from `dist/control/ray_cast_vehicle_controller.d.ts` in this repo's install):
  `addWheel(chassisConnectionCs, directionCs, axleCs, suspensionRestLength, radius)`, `updateVehicle(dt,
  filterFlags?, filterGroups?, filterPredicate?)`, and `currentVehicleSpeed`. Each wheel has setters
  and getters for `SuspensionStiffness`, `SuspensionCompression`, `SuspensionRelaxation`,
  `SuspensionRestLength`, `MaxSuspensionTravel`, `MaxSuspensionForce`, `FrictionSlip`,
  `SideFrictionStiffness`, `EngineForce`, `Brake`, `Steering`, `Radius` and axle/direction/connection.
  Read-only per wheel: `wheelIsInContact`, `wheelContactPoint`, `wheelContactNormal`, `wheelHardPoint`,
  `wheelGroundObject`, `wheelSuspensionLength`, `wheelSuspensionForce`, `wheelForwardImpulse`,
  `wheelSideImpulse` and `wheelRotation`. It has `indexUpAxis` / `indexForwardAxis`.
- Behavior: ray probes only. `frictionSlip × suspension force × dt` caps the combined
  (0.5 × forward, 1.0 × side) impulse, and past the cap the wheel is "sliding". Roll influence is
  fixed at 0.1 and not exposed. The brake works only when engine force is 0. Engine "force" is a
  force, not a torque. It reads no collider friction, has no low-speed handling, and does not use
  `currentVehicleSpeed` in friction. It lacks tire curves, wheel spin dynamics, a drivetrain,
  surfaces, anti-roll bars, shape casts, and Ackermann.
- Related primitives in 0.20: `world.castShape(...)` with `Cylinder` / `RoundCylinder` shapes,
  `RigidBody.applyImpulseAtPoint` / `addForceAtPoint`, and `setAdditionalMassProperties(mass, com,
  principalInertia, frame, wakeUp)`. Its doc says these values are **added** to the mass properties
  computed from colliders with non-zero density. Also `world.takeSnapshot()` and
  `World.restoreSnapshot(bytes)`.

## 2. Tire models

- **Slip.** κ = (r·Ω − vx)/|vx|, α = arctan(vy/|vx|). At low speed |vx| → 0 is a singularity, and the
  usual fix is an ε floor that depends on the step size (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slip_(vehicle_dynamics)).
- **Pacejka Magic Formula:** y = D·sin{C·arctan[Bx − E(Bx − arctan Bx)]}, where B, C, D and E are fitted
  constants (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_B._Pacejka). D is the peak, C the shape, B the
  stiffness and E the curvature (NWH's descriptions). It is empirical and needs measured data.
- **Brush model:** physically based and simplified, popular in the 1960s–70s. FTire, RMOD-K and
  CDTire are detailed physical models. Real-time FTire runs at kHz rates
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tire_model).
- **Simplified slip curves** used by engines: Unity (extremum and asymptote, 5 numbers), PhysX
  (3-point friction vs slip), Jolt (an arbitrary piecewise-linear curve), VPP (adherent, peak and
  limit over slip velocity), GTA V (`fTractionCurveMax` peak, `fTractionCurveMin` sliding,
  `fTractionCurveLateral` shape; https://gtamods.com/wiki/Handling.meta).
- **Combined slip:** a friction circle or ellipse that normalizes each slip by its peak and shares
  one μ. VPP and PhysX limit the combined force. Jolt's default does not, since each axis is capped
  separately.
- **Low-speed fixes:** (a) slip denominator floors (PhysX, Jolt); (b) relaxation-length transient
  slip (Bernard & Clover, SAE 950311: "comes to a stop in a reasonable way without numerical
  problems", https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/tire-modeling-low-speed-high-speed-calculations-950311);
  (c) velocity constraints at low speed (PhysX sticky tire, Jolt's constraint friction, VPP's
  adherent band); (d) substepping (Unity, PhysX).
- **Load sensitivity:** μ falls as load rises. Chaos exposes it as `WheelLoadRatio`, and PhysX as the
  lateral stiffness-vs-load graph and `loadFilter`.

## 3. Stability and assists

- **Suspension targets:** OptimumG puts passenger-car ride frequency at 0.5–1.5 Hz, with damping
  ζ ≈ 0.25 for comfort and 0.65–0.70 for race cars (https://www.scribd.com/doc/246048157/Springs-Dampers,
  a secondary copy). Defaults elsewhere: Jolt 1.5 Hz with ζ 0.5, Chaos ζ 0.5, PhysX ω 5–10 rad/s
  with ζ 0.8–1.2 (overdamped for robustness).
- **Anti-roll bars:** a spring on the difference of left and right compression. Stiffening the rear
  bar reduces understeer, and stiffening the front reduces oversteer (PhysX docs).
- **Center of mass:** Godot puts the COM at the body origin. Chaos and GTA V expose a COM offset
  (`vecCentreOfMassOffset`). Rapier needs it through additional mass properties (see 1).
- **ESC:** sensors for yaw rate, lateral acceleration, steering angle and wheel speeds. "To counter
  oversteer, it brakes the outer front wheel… understeer, the inner rear wheel". Some systems also
  cut engine power (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_stability_control). The reference yaw
  rate in the literature is r = v·δ / (L(1 + v²/v_ch²)), where v_ch² = gL/K_us comes from the
  understeer gradient, capped at |r| ≤ μg/v (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18422; US patent 8437936
  writes r = δ·v/(L + K_u v²)).
- **TCS/ABS:** Chaos has per-wheel `bABSEnabled` and `bTractionControlEnabled` (straight-line). NWH
  ships them as modules.
- **Arcade:** GTA V has `fLowSpeedTractionLossMult` (burnouts), `fTractionLossMult` (how much surface
  grip differs from 1.0), `fAntiRollBarForce`, `fRollCentreHeight*` ("closer to the center of mass,
  reducing body roll"), `fDownForceModifier`, `fCamberStiffnesss` and `fTractionBiasFront`. Jolt's
  `mMaxPitchRollAngle` is an anti-flip cone. In Rocket League the car's angular velocity is capped at
  5.5 rad/s (https://rlgym.org/Cheatsheets/game_values). Forza Horizon builds on Forza Motorsport's
  simulation, tuned for accessibility (https://gtplanet.net/forza-horizon-4-dev-interview-recreating-uk-weather-the-arcade-sim-spectrum).
  Its drift assists are undocumented.

## 4. Steering geometry

- Ackermann: the wheel axles point at a common center, so the inner wheel turns more. Production cars
  don't use pure Ackermann because of compliance and dynamic effects, and some race cars use reverse
  Ackermann (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ackermann_steering_geometry). For an ideal turn of radius R,
  wheelbase L and track t: tan δ_in = L/(R − t/2) and tan δ_out = L/(R + t/2). PhysX blends with
  `strength`.
- Maximum angles: Chaos 50°, Jolt 70°, GTA `fSteeringLock`. Unity's `GetWorldPose` and Rapier's
  `wheelRotation` give the visual pose. Chaos's `SuspensionSmoothing` trades smoothness for visual
  penetration.

## 5. Surfaces

Peak and sliding μ for a car tire (J.Y. Wong, *Theory of Ground Vehicles*, via
https://hpwizard.com/tire-friction-coefficient.html), with traction values from
https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/tractive-effort-d_1783.html:

| Surface | Peak μ | Sliding μ | Other source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asphalt/concrete dry | 0.80–0.90 | 0.75 | 0.9 (ETB) |
| Asphalt wet | 0.50–0.70 | 0.45–0.60 | 0.6 (ETB); Bosch 50 km/h new tread 0.65, 90 km/h worn 0.20 |
| Gravel | 0.60 | 0.55 | rolled dry 0.6–0.7, wet 0.3–0.5 (ETB) |
| Earth road dry / wet | 0.68 / 0.55 | 0.65 / 0.40–0.50 | dry clay 0.5–0.6 (ETB) |
| Loose sand | — | — | 0.3–0.4 (ETB) |
| Snow, hard-packed | 0.20 | 0.15 | dry ice/snow 0.2 (ETB) |
| Ice | 0.10 | 0.07 | wet ice 0.1 (ETB); black ice ≤ 0.10 (Bosch) |

A Swedish study found that a dusting of wet snow drops μ from 0.45 to 0.35
(https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_slipperiness). No primary measurement was found for grass or mud.

Rolling resistance Crr: car tires on concrete or asphalt 0.013 (Bosch), 0.010–0.015 (Wikipedia, ETB).
Rolled gravel 0.02, tarmacadam 0.025, unpaved road 0.05, field 0.10–0.35 (Bosch). Medium-hard soil
0.08 and sand 0.30 for passenger cars (Wong). Loose sand 0.2–0.4, and "solid sand, loose worn gravel,
soil (medium hard)" 0.04–0.08 (https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/rolling-friction-resistance-d_1303.html).
Sand 0.3 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_resistance). Snow (CRREL): about 0.017 below 22 cm
depth, about 0.05–0.12 at 13–21 cm depth (https://coldregionsresearch.tpub.com/CR95_07/CR95_070009.htm).

How engines model it: PhysX uses a tire-type × surface table. Jolt uses the geometric mean of tire
and body friction. Chaos uses `FrictionForceMultiplier` × material scale. GTA V uses
`fTractionLossMult` per vehicle. VPP's `groundGrip` comes per contact. Loose ground: VPP's flat
"limit" past the peak, and NWH's per-surface curves. Sinkage and drag are not built into any engine
above.

## 6. Drivetrain

- Torque curves are normalized over rpm (Chaos, Jolt, PhysX). Limiter at `MaxRPM`. Engine braking
  comes from Chaos `EngineBrakeEffect` or the PhysX zero-throttle damping rates.
- Clutch: PhysX solves engine, clutch and wheels implicitly (`eBEST_POSSIBLE`). In Jolt the clutch
  torque is ∝ `mClutchStrength` × (engine ω − average wheel ω at the clutch), zero in neutral, and in
  Auto it scales throttle by clutch engagement. NWH's clutch can stall the engine.
- Automatic shifting: Jolt uses absolute rpm (4000 up, 2000 down) plus `mSwitchLatency` 0.5 s. PhysX
  uses ratios of `maxOmega` plus `latency`. Chaos uses `ChangeUpRPM` / `ChangeDownRPM` without
  latency, and users report gear skipping.
- Differentials: open splits torque equally. Locked (a spool) holds equal speed. **A
  torque-sensing LSD's torque bias ratio (TBR) is the maximum ratio of the high-traction side's
  torque to the low side's**: 3:1 lets one side carry ¾. "If one wheel is lifted, the regular Torsen
  units would act like an open differential" unless they have preload or brake drag
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsen). Equivalent locking torque: slower side = T/2 + T_d/2, faster
  side = T/2 − T_d/2 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited-slip_differential). With TBR = b, T_d ≤
  T·(b − 1)/(b + 1). PhysX and Jolt "bias" and "limited slip ratio" values are **speed** ratios, a
  different quantity. A viscous LSD's coupling depends on speed difference.
- AWD center: Chaos `FrontRearSplit`, PhysX `centerBias`, Jolt `mDifferentialLimitedSlipRatio`, GTA
  `fDriveBiasFront`.
- Handbrake: a per-wheel torque on the rear wheels (Chaos 3000, Jolt 4000 N·m defaults).

## 7. Audio hooks

- Engine audio is loop-based. Fixed-rpm loops are crossfaded and pitched on an RPM parameter, and
  load has three states: on-load, off-load (no throttle, rpm above idle) and neutral (clutch out, at
  idle). On-load and off-load blend tracks should not crossfade at the same time
  (https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/loop-based-car-engine-design-with-wwise-part-2). AudioMotors in
  FMOD automates crossfade points
  (https://mcvuk.com/development-news/how-to-make-racing-car-engines-roar-using-audiomotors-fmod).
  BeamNG's `maxLoadMix` / `minLoadMix` blend off-load into on-load
  (https://documentation.beamng.com/modding/vehicle/sections/sounds/engine_audio/).
- Per-surface tire sound and effects come from NWH and Edy. The Chaos `SkidThreshold` /
  `SlipThreshold` gate skids. NWH ships 15+ effects: engine, transmission, suspension, forced
  induction, surface, crash and so on.

## 8. Timestep and networking

- Rates: Rocket League's physics runs at 120 ticks/s (https://rlgym.org/Cheatsheets/game_values), on
  Bullet. Forza Motorsport (2023) uses 8 tire contact points at 360 Hz, up from 1 point at about
  60 Hz (https://www.gamespot.com/articles/forza-motorsport-release-date-cars-and-everything-we-know/1100-6505944/).
  PhysX and Jolt take 60 Hz as the reference with vehicle-only substeps, and Unity substeps per vehicle.
- Rocket League: server-authoritative, as in Jared Cone's GDC 2018 talk "It IS Rocket Science!"
  (https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1024972/). Secondary accounts say clients predict the **whole world**
  (all cars and the ball) and re-simulate it on correction
  (https://discussions.unity.com/t/prediction-in-gameobject-netcode-devlog-entry-4/1719927). Remote
  players' inputs are predicted with decay (https://www.snapnet.dev/blog/netcode-architectures-part-2-rollback/).
  The world holds only six cars and a ball.
- GTA Online: peer-to-peer, and each networked entity has an owning client that simulates it. In
  FiveM/OneSync, `NETWORK_GET_ENTITY_OWNER` returns that client and ownership migrates; the server
  can lock entities (https://docs.fivem.net/docs/scripting-reference/onesync/). Rockstar documents
  nothing about driver-owns-vehicle.
- Unreal: Chaos physics prediction with resimulation (rewind and replay a frame window), and Modular
  Vehicles support it. It is experimental, and the 5.5–5.8 forum reports are mixed.
